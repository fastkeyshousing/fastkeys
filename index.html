Renting in the Netherlands is hard enough with a Dutch passport and a local phone number. From abroad, in a second language, against a thousand other students arriving the same week, it stops being a search and becomes a full-time job. FastKeys is the layer that takes it off you.

What we do

Four things stand between you and a front door.

We take all four.

The search

You send one message with your city, budget, dates and must-haves. We work through the listing sites, the agencies and the landlords we already know, and come back with a shortlist that actually fits — not forty links you have to filter yourself.

Viewings, without the flight

Still in your home country? We go instead. Video walkthrough of the room and the shared spaces, honest notes on damp, noise, the state of the kitchen, bike storage, and how long the ride to campus really takes.

The Dutch paperwork

We read the huurovereenkomst before you sign it: notice period, deposit rules, service charges, what happens if you leave early, and whether you can register at the address. Then we explain it to you in English, in plain words.

Move-in day

Key handover, meter readings and a photo record of the room's condition, so your deposit comes back at the end. After that we point you at the first practical things: your gemeente appointment, internet, insurance, a bike.

How we keep it safe

The rules we don't bend.

Housing scams target international students because they're far away, in a hurry, and can't verify anything. These four rules are how we make sure that isn't you.

No deposit to hold a room

Nobody — not us, not a landlord — should ask you to transfer money before you've seen a contract. If someone does, that's the end of the conversation.

You pay the landlord, not us

Rent and deposit go straight to the landlord on a signed contract, in your name, to an account we've checked.

Registration checked first

An address you can't register at costs you your BSN, your bank account and your health insurance. We confirm it before we recommend anything.

We tell you when it's bad

If a listing looks wrong, we say so — even when saying so costs us the placement. You get our honest read, not a sales pitch.

That's the situation we're built for. We view rooms in person on your behalf, film them, and send you the walkthrough the same day. You can sign and arrange everything from your home country.

We check this before we recommend a place. Registration with the gemeente (the BRP) is what gets you a BSN, and without a BSN you can't open a Dutch bank account, take a part-time job or arrange health insurance. Some landlords don't allow it — those places don't make our shortlist unless you specifically tell us you don't need it.

Usually one or two months' rent, paid to the landlord after the contract is signed. Anything above two months, or any request for money before a contract exists, is a warning sign. We photograph the room's condition at handover so there's evidence when you ask for the deposit back.

Typically a passport or ID, your proof of enrolment from the university, and proof of income or a guarantor — often a parent. Some landlords ask for a bank statement. We'll tell you exactly what a specific landlord wants before you apply, so nothing stalls at the last step.

Only for self-contained homes — your own front door, kitchen and bathroom — and only under income and rent limits set each year. A shared student room almost never qualifies. If a place might qualify, we'll flag it, but the application is yours to make with the Belastingdienst.

Earlier than feels necessary. For a September start, the good rooms move between April and July, and the last weeks of August are the worst time to be searching. If you're already inside that window, message us anyway — we work with what's left and we know where to look.
